What breaks a buying habit?

An exploratory analysis of a 2019 online-shopping dataset. We tested whether buying habits fade with tenure, whether coupons actually change basket size, and whether distinct customer types emerge from the data.

Authors: Tanja Kristín · Ýlfa Margrét
Course: DTU 02806 — Social Data Analysis and Visualization

The three main findings

The notebook followed three pillars: transaction timing, spending behaviour, and customer segmentation. These became the backbone of the website story.

1

Tenure did not explain habit decay

Activation rate stayed roughly flat across tenure buckets. The expected drop-off with customer age did not appear in the data.

2

Coupons mattered more than tenure

Basket size rose slightly when coupons were actually used, but deeper discounts did not keep increasing the effect.

3

Customers split into clear spending tribes

Low, medium, and high spenders behaved differently enough to support a segmentation story rather than a single average customer.

“The surprising result was not that discounts work a little. It was that tenure barely explained behaviour at all.”

The notebook’s strongest narrative move is the reversal of the original hypothesis. The website should keep that structure. Start with the expectation that older customers buy less often, then show the observed curve and explain why the pattern does not appear.

After that reveal, move the reader into the stronger signal: coupon behaviour. The heatmap and funnel can carry the middle of the story, because they are visually intuitive and easy to explain without much statistical background.

End with segmentation and a compact conclusion: habit did not break because of tenure alone, but behaviour still differs meaningfully across customer types and marketing responses.
